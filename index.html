Waqf Act 2025: Supreme Court Grants Partial Stay, Win for Owaisi and Other Petitioners — Entire Law Not Struck Down

The Supreme Court has delivered a significant interim ruling on a batch of petitions challenging the constitutional validity of the Waqf (Amendment) Act, 2025. A bench headed by Chief Justice B.R. Gavai, along with Justice Augustine George Masih, stayed several contentious provisions of the Act, granting partial relief to the petitioners — including AIMIM chief Asaduddin Owaisi. The petitions had argued that the amendment violated religious freedom under Articles 25 and 26 of the Constitution. Notably, the Court did not strike down the entire law — it only stayed the disputed provisions, meaning the Union government did not face a complete setback either. The matter remains pending before the Supreme Court, with a final verdict yet to come. The case continues to be at the center of a nationwide debate over the balance between the property rights of the Muslim community and the government's authority to regulate waqf properties.
